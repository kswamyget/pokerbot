# Omaha Poker: History, Culture, and Ecosystem (as of November 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-07. Every claim below has an inline source. Where sources conflict or are weak, that is noted. Several aggregator domains in the search results (for example deucescracked.com/blog, oddsreference.pages.dev, track360.io) look like SEO/AI content farms. I did not use them as primary sources.

## 1. Origins: Turner, Boyd, the Golden Nugget, naming, and European pot-limit roots

### Takeaway
Omaha reached Las Vegas casino play at the Golden Nugget in the early 1980s. Bob Ciaffone dates it to spring 1982; PokerNews says Turner approached Boyd in 1983. Robert "Chip Burner" Turner and a player named Gwen started the game, and cardroom chief Bill Boyd allowed it to be spread, briefly as "Nugget Hold'em." Within about a year the game moved to pot-limit and to England (1984), where British and Irish players made PLO their main "action" game.

### Cited Findings
- Robert "Chip Burner" Turner approached Bill Boyd, then Golden Nugget Director of Poker Operations, in 1983. He pitched a hold'em-like game with four hole cards. Boyd named it "Nugget Hold'em," and it was later renamed "Omaha Hold'em" and then "Omaha." — [PokerNews, "From the Poker Vaults: Bill Boyd" (June 2009)](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2009/06/from-the-poker-vaults-bill-boyd-6726.htm)
- Boyd had been moved from cardroom manager to Director of Poker Operations in 1982, after Steve Wynn's takeover. Boyd ran the Nugget cardroom from 1946 to 1988 and won four WSOP bracelets, all in five-card stud. — [PokerNews 2009](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2009/06/from-the-poker-vaults-bill-boyd-6726.htm); [Wikipedia: Bill Boyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Boyd_(poker_player))
- **Conflicting/earlier date:** Bob Ciaffone (Card Player, July 12, 2007) says Omaha came to the Golden Nugget in **spring 1982, right after the WSOP**. In his account Bobby Turner and "Gwen," a Seattle-area player nicknamed "The Dragon Lady," started a heads-up *limit Omaha high* game that soon filled and ran daily, with Boyd's permission. The Nugget marketed it as "Nugget hold'em" for only a short time. Pacific Northwest players called it "nine-card." — [Card Player, Ciaffone, "All About Omaha Origins"](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65671-erick-lindgren-20-12/articles/16829-all-about-omaha-origins)
- A 2006 PokerNews column also credits "Gwen" with bringing the game to the Nugget and questions claims that Turner brought it from Alabama. — [PokerNews, "Inside the Poker Tour (51) – The Naming of Omaha" (2006)](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2006/06/inside-poker-tour-51-2861.htm) (from search snippet only; not fetched)
- Wikipedia says the exact origin is unknown and credits Turner with first bringing Omaha into a casino. It explains that "hold'em" refers to the shared community cards and that the name reflects the rule of using exactly two hole cards. — [Wikipedia: Omaha hold 'em](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em)
- Antecedents: Ciaffone played a similar game in Michigan in the 1970s, called "two by three." James "Blackey" Blackburn had played it in Oklahoma. — [Card Player 2007](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65671-erick-lindgren-20-12/articles/16829-all-about-omaha-origins)
- **Shift to pot-limit:** The Stardust's "Stairway to the Stars" tournament in January 1983 featured *no-limit* Omaha with $5-$10-$25 blinds. About a week later the pros switched the game to pot-limit to slow down busts. After that, PLO became a regular side game at tournaments and was added as a tournament event at the Union Plaza, Caesars, and Vegas World. — [Card Player 2007](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65671-erick-lindgren-20-12/articles/16829-all-about-omaha-origins); [Card Player column](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1032200)
- Ciaffone wrote the first book on Omaha in 1984. Jack Keller had the best early PLO tournament record and won the 1984 WSOP Main Event. — [Card Player 2007](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65671-erick-lindgren-20-12/articles/16829-all-about-omaha-origins)
- **Europe/UK:** Keller promoted Omaha in England in 1984 with help from Don O'Dea, Derek Webb, and Derek Baxter. "The British and Irish quickly made it their favorite form of poker." London's Victoria Club ran a £1,000 buy-in game of half razz, half PLO that was "for a long time... likely the biggest sustained poker game in the world," with £10K–£20K swings. Blackburn and others introduced Omaha to Nepal in 1992. — [Card Player 2007](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65671-erick-lindgren-20-12/articles/16829-all-about-omaha-origins)
- In Europe "Omaha" usually means the high-only pot-limit version (PLO). PLO is popular in Europe, online, and in high-stakes mixed games in some US casinos. Fixed-limit Omaha/8 is the "O" in H.O.R.S.E. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em)
- Ciaffone: "more of the top pot-limit Omaha players are from Europe than anywhere else." Another Card Player column remembers PLO as "the action game" in 1990s UK cash games. — [Card Player 2007](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65671-erick-lindgren-20-12/articles/16829-all-about-omaha-origins); [Card Player](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1027962)
- **Variants:** Five-card Omaha ("Big O") reportedly appeared in Southern California in 2008; this is tagged "citation needed." Six-card Omaha is found in many UK casinos. Courchevel (five hole cards, one board card shown early) came from the Aviation Club de France, and PokerStars has offered it since 2013. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em)

### Inferences
- The most defensible framing is "early 1980s (1982–83), Golden Nugget, Turner plus Gwen, with Boyd approving." The single year varies by source: Ciaffone, a contemporary participant, says 1982; PokerNews 2009 says 1983.
- The original Nugget game appears to have been *limit*. Pot-limit came out of the 1983 Stardust tournament experience, and the UK/Irish scene (from 1984) made PLO the European standard.

### Gaps
- Turner's own account, "The origins of Omaha Hi/Lo poker" (Gaming Today, 2014), was not accessed.
- The date of the first WSOP Omaha event was not confirmed here.
- I found no sourced origin for the phrase "Omaha is the game of the future." Searches did not find it, so treat it as folklore unless sourced.
- I did not find a direct source for why the game was named after the city of Omaha. Wikipedia's explanation is about rules, not geography.

## 2. Rise of PLO in online poker (2000s high stakes)

### Takeaway
In 2008–2010 the Full Tilt Poker $500/$1,000 PLO tables became the biggest stage in online poker. Viktor "Isildur1" Blom's November 2009 run produced the largest online pots on record, mostly against Patrik Antonius and Phil Ivey.

### Cited Findings
- The largest online pot ever was ~$1,356,947, a $500/$1,000 PLO hand on Full Tilt Poker in November 2009, won by Patrik Antonius against Isildur1. Antonius bet $91K, Blom raised the maximum to $435K, and Antonius moved all in. — [PokerNews (Nov 2009)](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2009/11/patrik-antonius-and-isildur1-play-largest-ever-online-pot-at-7575.htm); [PokerListings Hand of the Week](https://www.pokerlistings.com/hand-of-the-week-so-long-isildur1-and-thanks-for-the-1-3m-pot-36132)
- A few days earlier Antonius and Isildur1 had set the record with an $878,958.50 pot. — [PokerNews (Nov 2009)](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2009/11/patrik-antonius-wins-largest-pot-in-online-poker-history-7551.htm)
- PokerNews's Top 5 Online Pots of 2009 noted that the biggest pots were almost all at the $500/$1,000 PLO tables on Full Tilt. — [PokerNews, Top Five Online Pots of 2009](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2009/12/the-top-5-online-pots-of-2009-7691.htm)
- Blom and Phil Ivey played a $1,127,955 PLO pot, reported at the time as the second-biggest online pot ever. — [PokerNews Top 5 of 2009](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2009/12/the-top-5-online-pots-of-2009-7691.htm)
- Isildur1 reportedly lost about $4.18M over 2,858 hands of PLO in one session, and reportedly about $3M to Antonius in a single day, at the time the biggest single-day win/loss online. Totals vary by source ($2.5M–$2.9M for the Antonius session). — [PokerNews Railbird Special (Nov 2009)](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2009/11/the-online-railbird-report-special-edition-antonius-wins-2-7-7557.htm); [Wikipedia: Viktor Blom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Blom)

### Inferences
- PLO's high variance and multiway equity made it the preferred nosebleed online game of that era. This reputation for "action and gambling" carries into the modern televised and live high-roller scenes.

### Gaps
- I did not verify specific online records or roles for Tom Dwan (the "Durrrr Challenge"), Gus Hansen, Jens Kyllönen, Ben Tollerene, or Phil Galfond (Galfond's "Galfond Challenges" and his Run It Once site, which ran 2019–2023, are well known but were not sourced in this pass). Black Friday (April 2011) ended the Full Tilt era; not sourced here.

At the 2026 WSOP, Michael Mizrachi won the $10K PLO Championship, Daniel Negreanu won the $100K PLO High Roller ($2.26M), and Eelis Pärssinen won the $25K PLO High Roller ($2.16M) plus a second bracelet. ## 5. Market data: PLO vs NLHE, regions, PLO5/PLO6, 2024–2026 trends

### Takeaway
I could not find any reliable public figure for PLO's share of online cash traffic or rake. The trackers I saw don't break traffic out by variant. A July 2026 PokerListings snapshot shows GGPoker dominating mid-stakes PLO seats, with PLO5 offered on several networks.

### Cited Findings
- PokerListings "Where to Play Omaha Cash Games Online in 2026" (updated July 24, 2026) took a seat snapshot on a Monday, Oct 13, 18:00 GMT; the year isn't stated, likely 2025. Occupied PL100 seats: GGPoker 105, CoinPoker 49, PokerStars 31, RedStar 10. Americas Cardroom had 21 at PL50, and WPT Global had 12 at PL50 and 28 at PL200. Typical rake is 4–5% with caps of $2–$4. WPT Global offers 30% rakeback on PLO and PLO-5. — [PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/blog/where-to-play-omaha-cash-games-online)
- The same publisher reports GGPoker had as many occupied seats as the next three rooms combined at the time observed. — [PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/blog/where-to-play-omaha-cash-games-online)
- Overall cash-traffic trackers (PokerListings, VIP-Grinders) report network-level totals, not a split by game. For example, VIP-Grinders' July 2026 report has GGPoker leading with about 9,726 cash players. — [PokerListings traffic analysis](https://www.pokerlistings.com/news/poker-room-cash-game-traffic-analysis-ggpoker-and-pokerstars-lead-the-pack); [VIP-Grinders](https://www.vip-grinders.com/?p=147640)
- Five-card Omaha is described as very popular as a home game in the Southeastern US. Six-card Omaha is found in many UK casinos. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em)

### Inferences
- Without variant-level tracker data, any claim like "PLO is X% of online cash traffic" should be treated as unverified. Seat snapshots suggest PLO is a meaningful minority of cash action, concentrated on GGPoker, with PokerStars and crypto rooms like CoinPoker as secondary pools.
- Asia's role shows up mostly in live high-roller PLO (Triton Jeju had record PLO fields in 2025, and the 2026 Jeju series paid more than $107M across 20 events), rather than in measured online data.

- I found no quantified growth figures for PLO5/PLO6 in 2024–2026. Affiliate claims (for example, "GGPoker leads PLO innovation with PLO5") are marketing, not data.

## 6. Culture

### Takeaway
Omaha's reputation as the "action" or gambling game is old and well documented. It dates to the 1983 Stardust no-limit tournament, the 1990s UK "action game," London's Victoria Club, and the 2009 Full Tilt nosebleeds. The "game of the future" slogan could not be sourced.

### Cited Findings
- The Stardust switched from no-limit to pot-limit after one week because players were busting too fast. — [Card Player 2007](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65671-erick-lindgren-20-12/articles/16829-all-about-omaha-origins)
- PLO was "the action game" in 1990s UK cash games. — [Card Player](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1027962)
- Swings of £10K–£20K were common in the Victoria Club game. — [Card Player 2007](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65671-erick-lindgren-20-12/articles/16829-all-about-omaha-origins)

### Inferences
- The variance reputation follows from the structure. Four hole cards give six two-card combinations, so equities run closer together and draws are bigger. That keeps PLO popular with recreational gamblers and the streamed nosebleed scene.

### Gaps
- No source was found for the attribution or origin of the "Omaha is the game of the future" catchphrase.
