# Omaha Poker: Rules, Structure, and Variants

## Q1. What are the exact rules of standard Omaha Hold'em, and what rule mistakes do players commonly make?

### Takeaway
Omaha is a flop game played like Texas Hold'em: blinds, a button, four betting rounds (preflop, flop, turn, river), a five-card board, and standard high-hand rankings. The difference is that each player gets four hole cards and must make a hand from exactly two of them plus exactly three board cards. Nearly every beginner misreading of a hand comes from breaking this "2+3" rule.

### Cited Findings
- Omaha uses a standard 52-card deck. Each player gets four hole cards and makes a hand from "exactly two of them, plus exactly three of the five community cards." A player cannot use four or five board cards, or three or four hole cards. — [Wikipedia: Omaha hold 'em](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em)
- Betting rounds and board layout (flop, turn, river) are the same as in Texas Hold'em. Up to 11 players can be dealt in. Six- or nine-handed tables are the most common. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em)
- Hand-reading consequences of the 2+3 rule:
  - A flush needs two suited cards in hand.
  - A straight cannot be made from one hole card and four board cards.
  - Two pair on the board does not give a full house unless the player holds a pair that matches a board card.
  - Trips on the board need a pair in hand to make a full house.

  — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em)
- Common mistakes:
  - Using three hole cards.
  - Thinking four hearts in hand plus two hearts on the board makes a flush. It does not, because only two hole cards can play.

  — [PokerSkill: Pot-Limit Omaha](https://www.pokerskill.com/blog/pot-limit-omaha/)
- With four hearts on the board, a player has no flush without two hearts in hand. A player also cannot "play the board" or use only one hole card. — [DeucesCracked: Omaha](https://www.deucescracked.com/learn/omaha) (secondary source)
- PokerNews states the two-and-three requirement with no exceptions. — [PokerNews: Introduction to Omaha](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/omaha-returning-base.htm); [PokerNews glossary](https://www.pokernews.com/pokerterms/omaha.htm)
- Nut awareness: K♥9♥ on a Q♥7♥2♣ flop is a non-nut flush draw, because any player holding A♥ with another heart makes a better flush. — [PokerSkill](https://www.pokerskill.com/blog/pot-limit-omaha/)
- Showdown rules from the TDA 2026 rules (Version 1.1, Sept 21, 2026):
  - Rule 13: "cards speak" decides the winner, and verbal declarations of hand value are not binding.
  - Rule 14-A: an Omaha showdown requires a player to table all four hole cards.
  - Rule 20: playing the board requires tabling all hole cards.

  — [Poker TDA Rules](https://www.pokertda.com/view-poker-tda-rules/)
- History:
  - Robert Turner introduced the game to Bill Boyd, who offered it at the Golden Nugget in Las Vegas as "Nugget Hold'em."
  - Super/System describes an early version with two hole cards.
  - Omaha 8-or-better is the "O" in H.O.R.S.E.

  — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em)

### Inferences
- Hand rankings are the standard high-poker rankings, since the game is "Hold'em with four hole cards."
- More mistakes follow from the same rule:
  - Three aces in hand plus one ace on the board is only trips, not quads.
  - On a board of A-K-Q-J-x, holding a single ten does not make a straight. The ten has to be paired with another hole card, and the hand then uses three board cards.
- "Playing the board" is impossible in Omaha. Ties happen only when players' 2+3 combinations rank equally.
- Some house rules require tabling all four cards, which matters because a dealer or player may misread an Omaha hand. TDA 14-A makes this the tournament standard.

## Q2. How does pot-limit betting work, how do no-limit and fixed-limit Omaha differ, and why does PLO dominate?

### Takeaway
In pot-limit, the maximum raise is the size of the pot after the raiser first calls. That gives the formula "raise to = 3 × the last bet + the pot before that bet." PLO dominates because four-card hands run close together in equity. In no-limit, that would turn the game into frequent preflop all-ins that are close to coin flips. The pot cap keeps the skill in postflop play.

### Cited Findings
- Formula for a full pot raise: total "raise to" = (POT + 2 × BET) + BET, where POT is the pot before the opponent's bet and BET is the opponent's bet. Worked example: a bet of 14.95 into a 29.9 pot.
  - 29.9 + 2 × 14.95 = 59.8, the pot after calling.
  - The raise is another 59.8, for a total of 74.75.

  — [PLO Mastermind help center](https://intercom.help/plo-mastermind/en/articles/12529822-how-to-calculate-raise-sizes-in-pot-limit-omaha-plo)
- Example with a 100 pot facing a 50 bet:
  - Calling 50 makes the pot 200.
  - The player can raise 200 more, for 250 total, which equals 3 × 50 + 100.

  — [PokerSkill](https://www.pokerskill.com/blog/pot-limit-omaha/)
- Shorthand: "raise to 3 × opponent's bet + the pot before that bet." Example: a pot of 10 facing a bet of 5 gives a maximum raise to 25. — [pokertraining.com: How to calculate the pot in PLO](https://omaha.pokertraining.com/poker/articles/beginners/how-to-calculate-the-pot-in-plo.php) (from a search snippet)
- TDA rules on pot-limit bets:
  - Rule 56-A: a player may ask for a pot count only in pot-limit games.
  - Rule 56-B: preflop, bets are calculated as if full blinds were posted.
  - Rule 56-C: postflop, bets are based on the actual pot.
  - Rule 56-D: "I bet the pot" is not a valid bet in no-limit.

  — [Poker TDA Rules](https://www.pokertda.com/view-poker-tda-rules/)
- Why pot-limit:
  - With four hole cards, preflop equities run close, so no-limit would mean frequent shoves that are nearly coin flips.
  - Capping bets at the pot keeps the early streets cheap and puts the skill in postflop play.
  - Example: AA with weak side cards against a T-9-8-7 rundown is only about a 6-to-4 favorite. In Hold'em, aces against a suited connector are about 4-to-1.

  — [PokerSkill](https://www.pokerskill.com/blog/pot-limit-omaha/)
- Because draws dominate and interfere with each other, "it's not uncommon for the nuts to be a significant underdog" in PLO. — [CardPlayer (via search snippet)](https://www.cardplayer.com/?p=1048628)
- Big draws: a "wrap" is a straight draw with nine or more outs, and four hole cards allow up to 20 outs. Pots grow quickly in pot-limit, so chasing draws gets expensive. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em)
- Which betting structures are used for each game:
  - PLO is popular in Europe, online, and in some American mixed games, and is usually played high-only.
  - Omaha Hi-Lo is usually fixed-limit, though pot-limit is growing.
  - Some low-stakes online tournaments use no-limit.

  — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em)
- PokerStars example: pot-limit Omaha and Hi/Lo games ran 6-max at stakes from 10c/25c to $200/$400. Fixed-limit Hi/Lo ran from $1/$2 to $1000/$2000. — [Pokerfuse (2013)](https://pokerfuse.com/news/poker-room-news/pokerstars-to-debut-poker-variants-five-card-omaha-courchevel-21-03/)
- Conflict: one forum snippet gives a 10c/20c preflop pot raise as 60c. That contradicts the standard formula, which gives 70c (3 × 20 + 10). — [PokerChipForum thread](https://www.pokerchipforum.com/threads/how-to-calculate-plo-pot-sized-bets-from-the-blinds-preflop.113051/). Treat the 60c figure as an error.

### Inferences
- Preflop worked example ($1/$2, first to act), applying the cited formula with the blinds counted as bets:
  - The pot is 1 + 2 = 3, and the call is 2, so the pot after calling is 5.
  - The player may raise 5 more, so a pot raise is to $7 (3 × 2 + 1).
  - If the next player re-pots: the pot is 1 + 2 + 7 = 10 and the call is 7, so the pot after calling is 17. The re-pot is to 24 (3 × 7 + 3).
- Postflop example: pot $20, Player A bets $20, Player B pots. B raises to 3 × 20 + 20 = $80.
- Fixed-limit Omaha: bets and raises come in fixed units, a small bet preflop and on the flop and a big bet on the turn and river, usually with a cap on raises. This is general knowledge consistent with the H.O.R.S.E. structure but not directly sourced here.
- No-limit Omaha: any bet up to a player's stack. It is rare because of the equity compression described above.
- The "raise to" versus "raise by" difference confuses many players. Online clients usually show "raise to."

### Gaps
- I found no primary source (such as Robert's Rules) giving a preflop pot-raise example. The $7 figure is derived from the cited formula.
- I found no authoritative market-share data on PLO's share of non-Hold'em traffic.
- The fixed-limit raise cap varies by house (often one bet and three raises) and is not sourced here.

## Q3. Omaha Hi-Lo (Omaha 8 or better): how does the qualifying low, split pot, quartering, and scooping work?

### Takeaway
The pot splits between the best high hand and the best qualifying low. A qualifying low is five unpaired ranks of 8 or lower, ace-to-five, with straights and flushes ignored. Each half of the hand is formed independently under the 2+3 rule. If no low qualifies, the high hand takes the whole pot. Ties on one side produce "quartering."

### Cited Findings
- Rules of the split:
  - The pot splits between the best high hand and the best qualifying low, and one player can win both.
  - The low must be five cards ranked ace-to-five, with no card above eight. A few casinos use a nine-low qualifier instead.
  - A player may use different hole cards for the high hand and the low hand.
  - If no low qualifies, the high hand wins the whole pot.
  - A low is possible only if the board has at least three different ranks of 8 or lower. A qualifying low appears about 60% of the time.
  - Quartering is common.
  - Straights and flushes do not count against a low. The best possible low is 5-4-3-2-A (the "wheel").

  — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em)
- An ace can count as both high and low. An eight-or-better qualifier applies in all high-low split games unless a sign posts otherwise (California cardroom rules filed with the state AG). — [CA AG game rules, e.g., BGC Omaha Hi-Low](https://oag.ca.gov/sites/all/files/agweb/pdfs/gambling/BGC_omaha_hi_low.pdf) (from a search snippet)
- There are two ways to scoop: hold the best high and the best low, or hold the best high when no qualifying low is possible. — search-result summary of [Pokerology Omaha High-Low](https://www.pokerology.com/poker/rules/omaha-high-low/)
- TDA Rule 21-C: in a high-low split, the odd chip in the total pot goes to the high side. Rule 21-A: when two or more players tie for high or for low in a board game, the odd chip goes to the first seat left of the button. — [Poker TDA Rules](https://www.pokertda.com/view-poker-tda-rules/)
- In WSOP mixed events, Omaha Hi-Lo 8 or Better is played fixed-limit (e.g., "Limit Omaha Hi-Lo 8 or Better" in the 2025 $1,500 Eight Game Mix). — [SoMuchPoker 2025 WSOP event listing](https://somuchpoker.com/events-calendar/wsop/2025/wsop-2025-las-vegas/73-eight-game-mix-6-handed-3-day-event-limit-holdem-limit-omaha-hi-lo-8-or-better-seven-card-stud-razz-seven-card-stud-hi-lo-8-or-better-no-limit-holdem-pot-limit-omaha-limit-2-7-lowball-triple-draw)

### Inferences
- How a low is formed: pick two unpaired hole cards of 8 or lower and three unpaired board cards of 8 or lower, all five ranks different. Compare lows from the highest card down.
  - Example: 7-5-4-2-A beats 7-6-3-2-A, because the first cards tie at 7 and the second cards compare 5 against 6.
- Example: holding A-2-K-K on a board of 3-4-8-K-J.
  - Low: A-2 + 3-4-8 makes 8-4-3-2-A.
  - High: K-K + K + two other board cards makes trip kings.
  - The player may scoop.
- Quartering example: two players hold the same nut low, and one of them also has the best high. That player wins 1/2 + 1/4 = 3/4 of the pot, and the other gets 1/4. When three players are in, a quartered player can lose money even while "winning."
- Counterfeiting: when a board card pairs one of a player's low cards, that player's low gets worse. For example, A-2 in hand loses value when an A or 2 lands on the board.

- I did not find sourcing on which regions use the nine-low qualifier.

## Q4. What other variants exist, and how do their rules differ?

### Takeaway
Most variants keep the 2+3 rule and change either the number of hole cards (five or six), the dealing order (Courchevel), the number of boards (double-board and bomb pots), or the low rules (Big O). Fusion grows from a Hold'em hand into an Omaha hand. Omaha Hi-Lo (fixed-limit) and PLO are both standard games in the WSOP 8-Game mix.

### Cited Findings
- **Five-card Omaha / PLO5:**
  - Players get five hole cards and must still use exactly two hole cards plus three board cards. Otherwise the rules match standard Omaha.
  - PokerStars launched the game alongside Courchevel on March 21, 2013. Before that, the "Big O" was dealt online only at Entraction, which closed in late 2012.

  — [Pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/news/poker-room-news/pokerstars-to-debut-poker-variants-five-card-omaha-courchevel-21-03/)
- **PLO5 and PLO6 generally:** these are "exactly the same as regular PLO but just with an extra hole card or two." 888poker launched PLO5 and PLO6 cash games in November 2024. — [PokerNews 2024](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2024/11/888poker-new-pot-limit-omaha-games-47335.htm)
- **PLO6 (six-card Omaha)** uses PLO5 rules with six hole cards (still 2+3), and PLO6 strategy is less developed. — [PokerListings](https://www.pokerlistings.com/poker-games/6-card-omaha-poker). Six-card Omaha ("6-O") is found in many UK casinos. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em)
- **PLO5 online:** GGPoker introduced 5-Card Omaha tables in 2021, and its client lists Omaha, PLO5, and PLO6. — [WorldPokerDeals](https://worldpokerdeals.com/blog/plo5-at-ggpoker-stakes-rake-features) (secondary source)
- **Big O:**
  - Big O is five-card pot-limit Omaha played hi-lo with an 8-or-better qualifier.
  - Players use exactly two hole cards plus three board cards, and may use different cards for high and low.
  - If no low qualifies, the high hand scoops.
  - Some California rooms also spread a high-only version.

  — [PokerSkill: Big O](https://www.pokerskill.com/blog/big-o-poker-rules-and-strategy/); [PokerNews: How to Play Big O](https://www.pokernews.com/poker-rules/big-o.htm)
- **Big O naming and regional history:**
  - Wikipedia uses "Big O" for five-card Omaha. It calls it a popular home game in the Southeastern US that began appearing in Southern California in 2008 (that claim is tagged "citation needed").
  - Pokerfuse uses "Big 'O'" for five-card Omaha in general.
  - Naming conflict: other sources (PokerSkill, Briggsoft) reserve "Big O" for the 5-card hi-lo version.

  — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em); [Briggsoft Poker Mavens docs](https://briggsoft.com/docs/pmavens/PMOmaha.htm)
- **Courchevel:**
  - Players get five hole cards.
  - The first board card is dealt face up before the first betting round. Two more cards then complete a three-card flop.
  - Players still use exactly two hole cards.
  - A hi-lo version exists.

  — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em); [Pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/news/poker-room-news/pokerstars-to-debut-poker-variants-five-card-omaha-courchevel-21-03/)
- **Double-board bomb pot (Omaha), per the Asian Poker Tour rulebook:**
  - Format: it can be played with 4, 5, or 6 hole cards, or as "Ultimate Hi-Lo" (best high and best low across both boards split the pot).
  - Ante and dealing: antes are collected before the deal, and there is no preflop betting.
  - Flop: burn one card, deal a three-card flop on the top board, then a three-card flop on the bottom board.
  - Turn and river: burn one card, then deal one card to each board.
  - Split: half the pot goes to the best hand on each board, and one player can scoop both halves.
  - Odd chips: in the general split, the odd chip goes to the top-board winner. When the split is on one board, it goes to the first active player clockwise from the button.

  — [Asian Poker Tour: Double Board Bomb Pot – Omaha](https://www.theasianpokertour.com/info/pokerhandbook/omahadoubleboardbombpot)
- **Bomb pots in general:** after all players post the required ante, the dealer goes straight to the flop with no preflop action. A player can end up with only a quarter of the pot by tying one board and losing the other. — [PokerTube](https://www.pokertube.com/article/what-is-a-bomb-pot-in-poker); [Poker-Project calculator page](https://poker-project.pages.dev/en/double-board-plo) (low-quality source)
- **Fusion (PokerStars):**
  - A six-max pot-limit game.
  - Players are dealt two hole cards as in Hold'em. Each player still in the hand gets a third hole card after the flop and a fourth after the turn.
  - The hand is made with exactly two hole cards plus three board cards, as in Omaha.
  - Introduced in late 2018. Reports conflict on whether it was withdrawn in 2019 or is back in the client.

  — [PokerNews 2018](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2018/11/pokerstars-fusion-game-32556.htm); [Pokerfuse](https://pokerfuse.com/news/poker-room-news/210303-pokerstars-takes-down-fusion-six-plus-holdem-could-make-its/); [PokerTube](https://www.pokertube.com/article/pokerstars-fusion-the-reality-behind-the-hype)
- **Omaha in mixed games:**
  - The 2025 WSOP $1,500 Eight Game Mix rotation: Limit Hold'em, Limit Omaha Hi-Lo 8 or Better, Seven Card Stud, Razz, Stud Hi-Lo 8 or Better, No-Limit Hold'em, Pot-Limit Omaha, and Limit 2-7 Triple Draw.
  - The 2025 $10,000 Eight-Game Championship used the same eight games.

  — [SoMuchPoker](https://somuchpoker.com/events-calendar/wsop/2025/wsop-2025-las-vegas/73-eight-game-mix-6-handed-3-day-event-limit-holdem-limit-omaha-hi-lo-8-or-better-seven-card-stud-razz-seven-card-stud-hi-lo-8-or-better-no-limit-holdem-pot-limit-omaha-limit-2-7-lowball-triple-draw); [PokerNews 2025 WSOP](https://www.pokernews.com/tours/wsop/2025-wsop/event-82-10000-eight-game-mixed-championship/day1/)
- **H.O.R.S.E.:** the "O" is Omaha 8-or-better. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha_hold_%27em)
- **2025 WSOP PLO events:** a $10,000 PLO Championship (8-handed) and a $50,000 PLO High Roller. — [PokerNews 2025 schedule](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2025/02/2025-world-series-of-poker-schedule-47958.htm); [Pokerology](https://www.pokerology.com/?p=7617)

### Inferences
- In PLO5 and PLO6, extra hole cards raise average hand strength and the number of nut draws. Because the 2+3 rule is kept, flushes and straights still need two hole cards. Players get the most confused about hand reading in these games.
- Dealer's Choice games typically include PLO, Omaha Hi-Lo, Big O, and double-board PLO, with the chooser setting the game and sometimes the structure. This is not directly sourced.
- Single-board bomb pots: the general bomb-pot description (ante, then straight to the flop) applies to single-board PLO bomb pots too.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable rules source for Short Deck (6+) Omaha or PLO variants. Short Deck is mainly a Hold'em variant (GGPoker lists "Short Deck" Hold'em), and I found no authoritative ruleset for a Short Deck PLO game.
- I could not confirm whether PokerStars currently offers Fusion as of November 2026.
- I found no official GGPoker or PokerStars help-page rules for PLO5 or PLO6. The sources are secondary.
- I found no official house rules for Dealer's Choice game lists.

## Q5. What rules are specific to heads-up play, tournaments versus cash games, dealing, misdeals, and running it twice?

### Takeaway
Heads-up, the button posts the small blind, acts first preflop, and acts last on every later street. The TDA rules (2026 v1.1, Rule 36-C) codify this, along with dead-button, misdeal, and odd-chip rules. Running it twice is a cash-game convention, common in PLO because equities run close. It needs heads-up all-in action and agreement from both players, and house rules vary.

### Cited Findings
- **TDA 2026 Rule 36-C (heads-up):**
  - The small blind is on the button.
  - The button acts first preflop and last on all later rounds.
  - The button may need adjusting at the start of heads-up play so no player takes the big blind twice in a row.

  — [Poker TDA Rules](https://www.pokertda.com/view-poker-tda-rules/)
- **Older TDA editions:** the 2011 rulebook says the heads-up button is dealt the last card. Rule numbers for this provision have changed across editions (#26, #34, #35 in various versions). — [TDA 2011 rulebook PDF](https://homepokertourney.org/docs/rulebook/poker-rule-book-tda-2011-fullsize.pdf); [TDA forum](https://pokertda.com/forum/index.php?msg=3779)
- **Dead button:** Rule 34 says tournaments use a dead button. Rule 36-B says the button is not tied to a seat and may skip empty seats so the blinds progress normally. — [Poker TDA Rules](https://www.pokertda.com/view-poker-tda-rules/)
- **Misdeals (TDA Rule 37):** a misdeal includes
  - two or more boxed cards on the initial deal,
  - the first card dealt to the wrong seat,
  - cards dealt to a seat not entitled to a hand, or a seat entitled to a hand being dealt out,
  - the wrong number of cards dealt to any player (Rule 39 excepted),
  - in flop games, cards exposed by dealer error.

  Under Rule 38, a misdeal cannot be called after substantial action unless the deck is fouled. A re-deal after a misdeal replays the hand exactly as before. — [Poker TDA Rules](https://www.pokertda.com/view-poker-tda-rules/)
- **Run it twice, typical cash-game rules:**
  - Only two players have live hands, at least one is all-in, and both agree.
  - The river has not yet been dealt.
  - Some games allow three or more runs.

  — [PokerNews: Running It Twice](https://www.pokernews.com/pokerterms/running-it-twice.htm); [Poker Wiki](https://poker.fandom.com/wiki/Running_it_twice)
- **Why it is common in PLO:** running it twice is "especially prevalent in Pot-Limit Omaha where the values of hands run so close together percentagewise." It reduces variance without changing expected value. Conventions vary by room, and one author says the only firm rule should be consistency. — [CardPlayer: Run It Twice](https://www.cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/65600-max-pescatori-19-20/articles/15723-run-it-twice); [CardPlayer: Vanessa Selbst on running it twice](https://cardplayer.com/cardplayer-poker-magazines/66213-vanessa-selbst-25-19/articles/20842-on-running-it-twice); [PokerNews strategy](https://www.pokernews.com/strategy/why-running-it-twice-does-not-affect-your-odds-32880.htm)
- **Online options:** Phenom Poker added Run It Twice and Run It Three Times in November 2025. GGPoker offers "All-In Poker Insurance," and I found no official GGPoker run-it-twice rule for PLO. — [Phenom changelog](https://phenompoker.featurebase.app/en/changelog/platform-updates-november-26th); [search summary of PokerNews 2019](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2019/07/pokerstars-all-in-cash-out-feature-insurance-protection-34983.htm)
- **The TDA rules do not cover running it twice.** — [Poker TDA Rules](https://www.pokertda.com/view-poker-tda-rules/)

### Inferences
- Heads-up Omaha uses the same blind and action order as heads-up Hold'em:
  - The button/SB posts the small blind and acts first preflop.
  - The BB acts first on the flop, turn, and river.
  - In pot-limit heads-up, the SB's maximum open is a raise to 3 × BB. With blinds 1/2, the SB completes to 2, which makes the pot 4, then raises 4, for a total of 6. That is less than the 7 a non-blind player could raise to at a full table, because the SB's own blind is already part of the call.
- Tournament versus cash: tournaments use a dead button and TDA rules, and running it twice is generally not used, because the TDA rules do not provide for it and chips have tournament (ICM) value. Cash games use house rules, which often allow running it twice and sometimes a moving button. Cash games often add straddles and bomb pots by house rule. Bomb pots and straddles are not sourced here as tournament features.
- Dealing in Omaha follows Hold'em procedure:
  - Cards are dealt one at a time clockwise from the small blind, four rounds of cards (five or six for PLO5 and PLO6).
  - The dealer burns one card before the flop, turn, and river.
  - The heads-up rule above sets who is dealt first.
- The burn-card procedure is confirmed for the double-board variant by the Asian Poker Tour rulebook.
